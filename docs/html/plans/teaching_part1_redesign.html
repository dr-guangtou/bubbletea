A Galaxy in Disguise: Part I implementation plan

Date: 2026-10-14
Status: Part I implemented and technically validated; final instructor review pending Task: BT-033; separate teaching work on teaching_ucd_course_20261012

Requirements · Course guide · Task register · Existing data provenance

1. Outcome and boundaries

Build one self-contained HTML file for Part I of a semester-long undergraduate project. It contains motivation, about 30 minutes of core reading, illustrated Gaia background, labeled examples, three practical tasks, and save/restore/submission controls. Students can spend longer on the investigations. No coding, installation, local server, account, or internet connection is required for the core activity. Online archive, survey, and literature exploration is encouraged but optional.

Implementation checkpoint, 2026-10-14: the full lesson is built, source evidence and all 74 cutouts are reviewed, and complete-page offline/performance/scoring tests pass. See the development record and instructor guide. The remaining gate is final instructor review of the actual reading, workload, and treatment of uncertainty. The earlier six-object HTML/PDF is version 1, not this redesigned lesson.

The audience already knows coordinates, magnitude, and color. Teach the additional background needed to investigate real observations. Replace most reading-comprehension questions with recorded decisions, source checks, and selection experiments. Allow and encourage LLM assistance with a brief disclosure and source verification.

Part II and later parts will address membership and new searches. Do not expand this implementation into choosing final discovery hosts, changing the scientific selector, modifying canonical classifications, or deploying the research workbench. The new student deliverable is HTML; the existing PDF remains a clearly labeled previous-version resource until a redesigned print companion is separately needed.

Final counts depend on exclusions and the finished HTML tests; 20,000 is a starting allocation, not a measured final sample size or sky abundance.

4. Scientific content and illustrations

Motivation

Explain UCDs as compact stellar systems spanning an imperfect boundary between massive star clusters and small galaxies. Some are candidate remnants of stripped galactic nuclei; do not claim that all share that origin. The accessible title is not a claim that every UCD has an unambiguous galaxy classification.

Explain that discoveries and searches have been concentrated in dense environments and that the census remains incomplete and selection-dependent. There is room to search around less-studied massive galaxies, disk hosts, and in lower-density environments. Do not invent a present-day global count, assert that every massive galaxy must host a detectable UCD, or equate an isolated host with a hostless UCD. Disk-host systems already exist: acknowledge SUCD1 near the Sombrero galaxy.

Useful primary sources for the authoring pass:

  • Saifollahi et al., Fornax outskirts: distinguish new candidates from confirmed objects and use its search-footprint discussion.
  • Liu et al., NGVS UCD catalog, DOI 10.3847/1538-4365/abad91: candidate richness, environment, and selection; its Figure 7 motivates the G-versus-flux-excess view, not a pre-taught cut.
  • Hau et al., SUCD1: a counterexample to a claim that no disk galaxy is known to host a UCD.

Background and Gaia

Cover angular versus physical size; apparent versus intrinsic brightness; uncertainty and significance; parallax and two-component proper motion; measurements versus source interpretations; matching catalogs at different epochs; missing data; and what images can and cannot resolve. Reinforce existing knowledge briefly.

Use an offline SVG or simple native animation of Gaia scanning across a small detector window, showing repeated observations at different orientations. Support pause, reduced-motion preferences, and a static explanation. Adapt from official mission documentation with a caption, source, and an explicit schematic label: ESA source detection and ESA scanning animation. Check reuse terms before embedding third-party imagery; do not depend on a remote video or iframe. An original, credited schematic is the simpler reliable default.

Explain why compact peaks of light can be recorded without determining whether the object is a Galactic star. Distinguish pixel sampling, direction-dependent angular resolution, close-pair catalog recovery, and astrometric precision. Never offer one universal Gaia resolution or a size threshold guaranteeing UCD detection. Reuse the verified primary-source resolution references in the existing background guide, checking definitions during rewriting.

Present neutral definitions, units, and missing-value meanings for each control. Do not label a feature as useful/useless, give a preferred cut direction, or preload the research selector. Link the official Gaia DR3 source data model and the repository feature definitions as optional deeper reading. Optional instructor-oriented repository pages must not be required for understanding the offline lesson.

Labeled examples

Begin with M60-UCD1 and/or M59-UCD3 and magnitude-comparable, independently classified star and QSO examples. Select more than one comparison if a single easy star would suggest a false universal rule. Show the same measurement layout used in Task 1, two image scales, exact identifiers, units/errors, and source links. Keep example objects disjoint from both tasks so the answer is not copied by recognizing an image. Use the existing tool guide for optional Gaia Archive, Visualization, Aladin, and Legacy Survey exploration; verify changed interface instructions rather than claiming a Windows rehearsal.

6. Task 1 interface and grading

Use neutral object codes in a compact navigator and one detail panel with images, measurement table, optional links, and response fields. Preserve RA/Dec and exact Gaia identifiers for open investigation. Do not put class-bearing names in display titles, image captions, alternative text, download filenames, or hidden Task 1 data. Reference labels and the audit/key remain in a separate instructor artifact. External links may legitimately reveal a published identity: this is allowed.

Response fields: class (ucd, star, qso, uncertain, or unanswered), confidence (low, medium, high, or unanswered), one short evidence note, evidence basis (supplied_observations, external_record, or both), and a source URL or citation. Under the revised 2026-10-14 request, only classification is mandatory. Use button-style radio choices for class, confidence, and evidence basis. All other fields are optional, including citations. Show Galactic longitude/latitude on the object cards, computed from the saved ICRS positions; do not use the coordinate system as a suggested selection shortcut. Keep the per-object form compact.

Layout follow-up, 2026-10-14: measurements are above the review area in two columns on wider screens. The close/context images sit beside a compact response panel containing the three choice groups, evidence, citation, and navigation. The image column stays visible while scrolling the response panel on desktop-sized windows. Previous/next brings the new images and classification back into view without returning to the long measurements. Narrow screens stack images before responses; the measurements become one column on the narrowest screens. Preserve the example cards, all measurements, classification options, and the existing answer schema.

Show progress but no correctness, accuracy, class-colored symbols, or answer distribution. Grade against the separate reference key plus the evidence notes; do not automatically equate a cautious, justified uncertain answer with failure. Record questionable reference cases for instructor review. Never promote student responses into canonical research labels.

7. Task 2 measurements, controls, and feedback

Data presentation and adjustable rules

Use unlabeled points initially. Required view: G magnitude versus (I_BP + I_RP) / I_G, the Gaia BP/RP flux-excess factor. Add G versus astrometric excess noise, BP−RP versus flux excess, and parallax significance versus combined proper-motion significance. Explain log axes and non-plottable values. A source missing a plotted coordinate remains in the catalog, selection, and denominator; show each panel's omitted count. Do not silently replace missing values with zero.

Expose neutral, optional lower/upper interval controls for G, BP−RP, flux excess, astrometric excess noise, its reported significance, RUWE, absolute-parallax significance, combined proper-motion significance, and the two image-window fractions. Keep the more technical fields in an expandable section. The existing frozen logistic model uses most of these quantities; this exercise does not ship its coefficients, predictions, labels-as-features, or optimized thresholds. Do not promise that a particular field is unimportant in advance. The instructor audit should establish which controls add little information in this actual sample; do not invent random or identifier-based decoys to force that outcome.

All cuts start disabled; their displayed domains come from measured data. Enabled intervals combine with AND: a source must satisfy every active rule. Inclusive endpoints and units are explicit. One clearly labeled missing-data control chooses whether unavailable values pass active rules; start with keep, report their count, and save this policy with every attempt. A log-spaced slider is only an input aid: store and apply the corresponding physical-unit boundary. Provide numeric inputs, keyboard access, reset, and visible pending changes. Moving a slider does not replace the last evaluated result until the student presses Evaluate selection.

Derived features must match the verified definitions:

  • BP−RP is the difference between the two magnitudes, not a flux ratio.
  • Absolute-parallax significance is abs(parallax) / parallax_error, requiring finite values and a strictly positive error. It is a comparison with zero, not a posterior probability or a parallax-based UCD distance.
  • For proper motion, set u = pmra / pmra_error, v = pmdec / pmdec_error, and rho = pmra_pmdec_corr; use sqrt((u*u + v*v - 2*rho*u*v) / (1-rho*rho)) when errors are positive, all inputs finite, and abs(rho) < 1. Otherwise report unavailable. It is a two-dimensional error-scaled distance from zero, not a one-dimensional Gaussian tail probability. Keep the full formula in optional mathematical detail.

Feedback and one final selection (revised 2026-10-14)

After evaluation, highlight selected points without automatically revealing each point's class. Show aggregate counts for selected secure UCDs, stars, quasars, unconfirmed candidates, and selected objects with missing active measurements.

Let true_positive be selected securely classified UCDs, false_positive be selected spectroscopic stars plus quasars, and total_ucd be all eligible secure UCDs in the fixed Task 2 reference sample, including rows with missing features:

  • purity = true_positive / (true_positive + false_positive);
  • completeness = true_positive / total_ucd;
  • f1 = 2 * true_positive / (total_ucd + true_positive + false_positive).

An empty scored selection has undefined purity, not 100%; completeness is zero when a nonempty UCD reference sample exists; F1 is also zero. Guard all zero denominators. Unconfirmed candidates enter none of these formulas. Label purity as reference-subset purity, not the known purity of the entire selected list: show unknown selected candidates alongside it. Explain that these metrics describe this constructed catalog, not discovery success or an unbiased estimate for a new galaxy field.

Keep evaluated attempts with their actual rules, counts, metrics, and order. Allow repeated attempts; avoid logging every slider movement. Students optimize F1, the harmonic mean of purity and completeness, and designate one final attempt. Require its answers to "Why do you think this works?" and "How do you think this selection can be improved?" Do not impose a numerical pass threshold. This supersedes the earlier two-list design. Retain range controls, not scoring weights; ROC AUC is optional background, not the objective for a binary selection. The instructor-only bounded trial tests achievable F1 and all/none/one-object controls without placing optimized cuts in the student page or claiming held-out validity.

8. Task 3 and responsible assistance

Use exactly one prompt:

You find a possible UCD close to a massive galaxy on the sky. How would you investigate whether it is physically associated with that galaxy?

One free-text box; no hint panel, worked solution, suggested answer, or automatic score. Do not inadvertently give its worked answer in the background or concluding section. Save it for the instructor to use at the start of Part II.

At submission, ask whether an LLM was used and, if so, for a brief description of its contribution and what the student checked. Do not request private chat logs or treat tool use as misconduct. Assess observational reasoning and reproducible work, not whether prose could have been generated by a model.

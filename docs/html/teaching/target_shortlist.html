Their missing measurements must not be entered as zero.

Why retain M60-UCD1 as the main example?

It combines published physical characterization, a relatively bright Gaia record, and unusual Gaia fit/flux measurements without significant measured motion. Strader et al. (2013) adopted 16.5 Mpc and measured a 24.2 pc half-light radius. It is close to M60's light, so it also teaches why context matters. The current DR11 cutout shows the compact source near its center, a strong background gradient, and nearby green streaks. Do not interpret those streaks as part of the UCD or present the image as artifact-free.

This is a known-object teaching exercise, not a claim that the frozen M60 host search recovered this famous object. The discovery paper places it only 6.6 kpc in projection from M60, inside the current conservative inner search exclusion. It is not one of the two reliable controls counted in that outer-profile run.

M59-UCD3 is brighter in Gaia and its checked close-up is less dominated by host background. Its discovery paper is Sandoval et al. (2015). Use it alongside M60, or as the first image if the class needs a cleaner-looking demonstration. Gaia alone did not establish either object's physical nature.

Before assignment, the instructor must check the original publications and table rows, Gaia match, image center, brightness, host context, external associations, and DR11 image quality. Select additional varied examples from the existing reference bundle, including clear and ambiguous appearances, rather than only easy or attractive sources. Keep confirmed examples and candidates labeled separately. This exercise is not a representative contamination-rate measurement.

3. Possible new search hosts: not yet an approved sample

The inherited nearby-galaxy pilot table supplies a first luminosity-ranked list. K-band luminosity is useful as a rough indicator of stellar mass, not a measured stellar mass. The following are plausible fields to investigate, not certified hosts with no published UCDs and not the three most massive galaxies in the nearby Universe.

Host RA, Dec (degrees) Inherited distance (Mpc) Inherited absolute K magnitude Rationale and caveat
NGC 2974 (also NGC 2652) 145.6387045, −3.6991485 22.428 −25.499 Luminous in the inherited table; in SLUGGS but not its UCD table. First practical field to audit; verify the unusually high luminosity against independent photometry/mass before calling it a top-mass host.
NGC 1395 54.62447083, −23.02747222 24.277 −25.032 Rich published globular-cluster system, so scientifically interesting; group environment complicates membership. Published work did not identify a significant bright massive-cluster/UCD population, which is not a claim of none.
NGC 4753 193.091951, −1.1996105 24.479 −25.222 Published study reported no UCD candidates under its criteria. Useful recovery/null-result exercise; existing negative evidence is a caveat for discovery yield, not proof the wider halo is empty.

Distances and magnitudes are rounded values read from pilot_sample_top100.csv, not newly adopted distance measurements. Check distance-source consistency before luminosity cuts: SLUGGS uses 20.9 Mpc for NGC 2974, for example, and the NGC 1395 study derives 21.1 Mpc from its cluster luminosity function. Different adopted distances change inferred luminosity and projected separation.

What the literature check actually establishes

  • Forbes et al. (2017), SLUGGS, includes NGC 2974 but does not list it among its separately tabulated UCD hosts. This is bounded evidence, not an exhaustive absence claim.
  • Escudero et al. (2018) finds a rich NGC 1395 cluster system but no significant population of bright massive-cluster/UCD candidates in its color-magnitude analysis. The study's footprint and selection matter.
  • Caso et al. (2015), Section 3.1, reports no UCD candidates around NGC 4753 using its adopted magnitude/color criteria and studied fields. A Gaia search would test a different, explicitly limited sample.

Each has zero exact-host-label associations in our current canonical compilation; that check is insufficient on its own. A counterexample is NGC 2768: it has zero under this exact-label test but does have a published UCD in the SLUGGS table. The script's hardcoded is_lit_host flag is therefore not an eligibility criterion. The source-check record gives the bounded ADS query and limits.

Imaging and Gaia eligibility are still separate checks

The standard ls-dr11 service returned nonzero g/r/z FITS pixels at the centers of all three hosts, with nonuniform wider JPEGs also checked numerically for NGC 2974 and NGC 4753. NGC 1395's first request was rate-limited, then succeeded on a serial retry. These verify small center samples, not complete usable coverage of any search annulus. No host-specific HSC footprint has been verified. HSC is optional, and Subaru imaging in a paper does not automatically mean HSC public-release coverage.

DR11 is a release, not a guarantee of new observations everywhere: the northern part reuses DR9 reductions. See the official DR11 description and HSC PDR3 coverage tools.

At these distances we cannot promise Gaia detects every real UCD. The actual detections above verify specific luminous examples, not completeness for a host population. Before selecting a class field, measure recovery of relevant known objects, inspect magnitude/measurement availability, and verify the chosen radial area's DR11 imaging. Preserve radial rings and exclude a justified inner galaxy region; do not adopt the image cutout size as a search aperture.

Recommendation: M60-UCD1 and M59-UCD3 for the first session; a small published candidate packet for student contributions; NGC 2974 as the first host to finish auditing, NGC 1395 as an interesting alternative, and NGC 4753 as a useful published-null comparison. Do not start a new-host class search until those remaining checks pass.

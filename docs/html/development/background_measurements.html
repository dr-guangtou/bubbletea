Large-sky background model comparison

Date: 2026-07-19

Script: scripts/phase3_background/freeze_large_sky_model_comparison.py

Machine-readable contract: data/results/background/large_sky_model_comparison_contract_v1.json

Contract identifier: model_contract_8da80ba5e4bd6d3027e6

What is being predicted

For each field, the measured quantity is the number of sources passing the frozen Gaia selector per square degree of successfully queried area. The model works with the count and exact area together so that fields with more measured area carry the appropriate statistical weight. An incomplete field keeps a failure record and is left out of fitting; it is never changed to a zero count.

Models to compare

The models are ordered from simplest to most flexible:

  1. One average rate for the entire development sky.
  2. A rate that can change smoothly with distance from the Milky Way plane.
  3. A sky-position model that also uses Galactic hemisphere and longitude, plus distance from the ecliptic plane.
  4. The position model plus measured Gaia conditions: total Gaia source density, M10, mean visibility periods, mean matched transits, and three summaries of the G-magnitude distribution.
  5. A limited tree model using the same information. This checks whether simple nonlinear relationships improve prediction enough to justify a less transparent model.
  6. A diagnostic version that adds the count-blind environment flag. This comparison cannot approve or delete a background field and cannot be selected as the main model.

A smooth curve is used where a straight-line relationship would be unnecessarily restrictive. The allowed curve complexity and the limited tree settings are fixed in the machine-readable contract before the 398-field measurements.

How prediction is tested

The 398 development fields belong to 95 broad sky blocks. All fields in one block stay together throughout testing, which prevents nearby fields from appearing on both sides of the same test.

  • An outer grouped fold holds out whole development sky blocks, fits the model on the other blocks, and measures how well it predicts the held-out blocks. Five different outer splits provide the performance estimate.
  • An inner grouped fold operates only inside the current outer training set. Its sole job is to choose among the already declared curve, penalty, or tree settings. Four inner splits are used. Inner results are not reported as final performance.

The main score is mean Poisson deviance, which compares predicted and observed counts while respecting the count nature of the data; lower is better. Supporting checks report typical and root-mean-square log-density errors, residual patterns by Galactic and ecliptic latitude, and whether the stated 68% and 95% prediction ranges contain the expected fractions of held-out fields.

Choosing the final development model

The lowest average outer-fold deviance identifies the measured best score. The chosen model is the simplest candidate whose score is statistically indistinguishable from that best score under the predeclared one-standard-error rule. In plain language, a more complicated model is used only when its improvement is larger than the field-to-field uncertainty in the comparison.

After selection, the development fit, all data transformations, and the method for constructing prediction ranges are frozen. Only then may the 102 held-back fields be opened once for a final check. They cannot be used to revise the model.

Current status

The frozen comparison has now been run on all 398 development fields. The selected model combines sky position with measured Gaia conditions and uses three spline knots with alpha = 0.1. All 22 fitting safeguards pass. The extreme flagged field sky_field_0317 is retained and documented rather than deleted; it dominates one outer fold and limits the model's applicability in unusual crowded or physically associated regions. No validation selector result has been opened, and no large-sky field has been approved as a scientific background. See docs/large_sky_development_model_comparison.md.

After reviewing the extreme-field failure, this fitted model was reassigned on 2026-08-11 to a secondary diagnostic role. It can describe smooth trends, but it does not supply the primary background value. The primary large-sky reference is now the preserved distribution of directly measured development fields in the same broad absolute-Galactic-latitude band. See docs/large_sky_direct_background_comparison.md.

Large-sky development model comparison

Date: 2026-07-19

Script: scripts/phase3_background/fit_large_sky_development_models.py

Command: uv run python scripts/phase3_background/fit_large_sky_development_models.py

Input: data/results/background/large_sky_development_scale_summary_v1.csv

Machine-readable result: data/results/background/large_sky_development_model_comparison_v1.json

Outcome and revised role

The selected development model combines sky position with measured Gaia observing conditions. It uses Galactic and ecliptic latitude, Galactic hemisphere and longitude, total Gaia source density, the local depth indicator called M10, mean visibility periods, mean matched transits, and three summaries of the Gaia G magnitude distribution.

The final setting has three spline knots and alpha = 0.1. A spline is a smooth curve assembled from connected pieces; three knots allow a modest curved latitude trend without a highly flexible shape. Alpha is the strength of a penalty that discourages unnecessarily large regression coefficients. It is a model-stability setting, not a source-selection cut.

This model is now a diagnostic interpolation only. Directly measured random-field distributions are the primary large-sky reference; see docs/large_sky_direct_background_comparison.md. The fitted model remains useful for showing smooth trends and as a robustness comparison, but it does not replace the measured comparison-field densities.

All fields in one broad sky block stayed together. Each score therefore measures prediction on sky blocks that were not used to fit that fold or choose its setting. Lower mean Poisson deviance is better.

The Gaia-condition regression has the lowest eligible score. Its one-standard-error threshold is 625.326; no simpler eligible model lies below that threshold. The environment-flag version has a similar score but was declared diagnostic before measurement and cannot become the primary model.

Prediction ranges

Grouped predictions for fields excluded from their current fit give these frozen observed-to-predicted multipliers:

  • central 68% range: 0.858 to 1.174;
  • central 95% range: 0.685 to 1.374.

For example, a prediction of 100 passing sources per square degree corresponds to a development residual range of about 86--117 for the central 68% and 68--137 for the central 95%. These are empirical field-to-field ranges from the development sample. They are not yet independently validated confidence intervals.

Important instability

Four outer test folds have mean Poisson deviances between 14.030 and 18.337. The fifth has 1,539.116 because it contains an extreme development field, sky_field_0317. That field has an observed passing-source density of 37,714.863 per square degree, while the model fitted without its sky block predicts 3,265.938 per square degree. It is marked by the pre-existing count-blind environment flag and was retained exactly as required; it was not converted to a failure, removed, or used to alter the frozen comparison.

This one field also drives the large residual summaries in Galactic equal-area band 1 and the 75--90 degree absolute-ecliptic-latitude band. When the selected model is tested only on the 163 unflagged development fields, its mean outer-fold deviance is 66.522 with a standard error of 57.344. That sensitivity result is substantially lower but remains diagnostic because excluding flagged fields was not allowed to choose the primary model.

The practical conclusion is that Gaia observing conditions explain most ordinary development-field variation, but the model does not absorb every extreme crowded or physically associated sky region. The eventual applicability rule must retain this limitation rather than present one smooth prediction as universally reliable.

Safeguards and status

The local 60-field synthetic comparison passed all ten checks before the real fit. The full comparison completed in 220.35 seconds with all 22 safeguards passing and no convergence warning. All transformations were fitted within training data, all six frozen model forms were evaluated, and residuals were recorded by Galactic band, ecliptic band, and environment-flag state.

The fitted development model and its prediction ranges are now frozen. No field is approved as a scientific background, and selector results for all 102 held-back validation fields remain unopened.

Reserved-field diagnostic result

The earlier plan to validate this fitted model as the primary background procedure was superseded on 2026-08-11. The 102 reserved fields were later opened once to evaluate the frozen direct procedure, with this serialized model included only as a secondary diagnostic. Its validation typical difference is a factor of 1.128; 66/102 fields lie inside its frozen 68% range and 100/102 inside its frozen 95% range. It was not refitted or retuned and remains secondary. See docs/large_sky_validation_run.md.

Direct random-field background comparison

Date: 2026-08-11

Contract script: scripts/phase3_background/freeze_large_sky_direct_background_contract.py

Comparison script: scripts/phase3_background/compare_large_sky_direct_backgrounds.py

Input: data/results/background/large_sky_development_scale_summary_v1.csv

Machine-readable result: data/results/background/large_sky_direct_background_comparison_v1.json

Why this replaced a model as the primary reference

The density of Gaia sources passing the frozen selector can be measured directly in random sky fields. A fitted mathematical curve is therefore not required to supply the primary large-sky background reference. The curve remains useful as a secondary check, but it can hide extreme fields behind a smooth prediction.

The direct procedure preserves the actual comparison fields and their measured densities. It reports a median, an arithmetic mean, and central 68% and 95% ranges. These ranges show real field-to-field variation; they are not model confidence intervals.

Procedures compared

The rules were frozen before their performance was measured:

  1. use all training fields in the same broad absolute-Galactic-latitude band;
  2. additionally require the same low, middle, or high third of total Gaia source density; or
  3. use the 20 nearest training fields in Galactic latitude and total Gaia source density.

All fields in the same broad sky block stayed together. A field was therefore tested only against comparison fields outside its held-out block.

The latitude-band procedure is selected by the frozen one-standard-error rule. Its score differs from the best measured score by only 0.0010, while it is simpler, uses roughly three times as many comparison fields, and gives better measured-range coverage. Its overall typical absolute error corresponds to a density factor of 1.182, or about an 18% difference between a held-out field and the comparison-pool median.

The central ranges do not contain exactly 68% and 95% of held-out fields. Their measured coverage is 65.6% and 91.0%, respectively. This shortfall must remain visible and can be checked once on the still-unopened validation fields.

Extreme flagged field

sky_field_0317 has a measured passing-source density of 37,714.863 per square degree. Its 58 same-latitude comparison fields have a median of 390.433 and a central 95% range of 177.324--659.385 per square degree. The field is therefore a factor of 96.597 above the median and far outside the measured range.

This is more informative than forcing a smooth prediction: the direct procedure plainly identifies the field as unlike ordinary random fields in its latitude band. The field remains preserved with its count-blind environment flag. It is not deleted, converted to zero, or allowed to redefine the general background.

Interface with a real host

For each real galaxy, the future result must report both:

  • the density measured in a valid outer annulus around the galaxy; and
  • the median, mean, central ranges, and field count from the direct random-field reference.

It must also report the annulus density minus the random-field median and their ratio. Neither measurement is forced to agree with the other. A missing or invalid outer annulus remains unavailable, not zero.

The random-field reference applies only to |b| >= 20 degrees, the domain actually measured by the frozen grid. The real outer-annulus boundaries and central excluded region still require separate calibration before the official host run.

Status

The synthetic 300-field validation passes all ten safeguards in 0.666 seconds. The 398-field comparison passes all 17 safeguards in 1.405 seconds and makes no archive query. All comparison-field identities and measured densities are retained. The fitted model is now a diagnostic only. No field is approved as a scientific background. At this design-freeze point, all 102 large-sky validation fields were still unopened for a new branch.

The reserved fields were subsequently opened once under the unchanged procedure. The direct validation typical difference is a factor of 1.197, with 63/102 fields inside the development 68% measured ranges and 94/102 inside the 95% ranges. The procedure was not retuned and remains the primary large-sky reference. See docs/large_sky_validation_run.md.

One-time large-sky validation run

Date: 2026-08-11

Contract script: scripts/phase3_background/freeze_large_sky_validation_run.py

Retrieval script: scripts/phase3_background/run_large_sky_validation_scale.py

Contract: data/results/background/large_sky_validation_run_contract_v1.json

What is being tested

The 102 reserved fields are fixed sky positions whose Gaia selector results were not used to choose the background procedure. They provide a one-time test of how well that already chosen procedure works on new parts of the sky.

The primary procedure is direct measurement. For each reserved field, it uses the 398 development fields in the same broad absolute-Galactic-latitude band as the comparison sample. The comparison sample supplies its measured median, mean, and central 68% and 95% ranges. It is not a fitted prediction.

The previously fitted sky curve will also be evaluated from the same retrieved measurements, but only as a secondary diagnostic. It will not be refitted, and its performance cannot change the primary procedure.

Rules frozen before the first query

  • The contract contains all 102 field identifiers in a count-blind processing order. Count-blind means the order was computed from identifiers, not Gaia source counts or selector results.
  • Retrieval uses the unchanged 19 selector inputs, frozen selector version, exact circular area, 5,000-row strip limit, 180-second request limit, and 300-second whole-field attempt limit.
  • The default command retrieves zero new fields. Every live run requires an explicit upper limit on the number of fields attempted.
  • Completed fields keep verified raw checkpoints and a receipt outside the repository under BUBBLETEA_EXTERNAL_DATA.
  • A failed or interrupted field keeps a failure receipt and remains missing. It is never recorded as zero sources.
  • Environment flags are reported but cannot remove fields from the primary result.
  • Neither the direct procedure nor the fitted diagnostic may be retuned after a reserved result is opened.

Frozen measurements to report

For both the direct procedure and the fitted diagnostic, report:

  • the typical absolute log-density difference;
  • the root-mean-square log-density difference;
  • the fraction of reserved fields inside the frozen 68% range; and
  • the fraction inside the frozen 95% range.

Also preserve every field-level result, list the ten largest absolute differences, and compare validation performance numerically with development performance.

During development, each field was tested against a reduced pool because its whole sky block had to be excluded from training. Validation uses all 398 already frozen development measurements because every reserved sky block is separate from them. The resulting validation pools contain 75--85 fields. The comparison therefore tests the final deployed reference rather than recreating the smaller development folds.

For the direct method, validation minus development is +0.013045 in median absolute log difference, -0.098336 in root-mean-square log difference, -3.81 percentage points in 68% coverage, and +1.20 percentage points in 95% coverage. The validation 95% coverage is closer to 95% than the development value, although it remains below 95%; no range was changed after seeing this result.

The eight direct results outside the frozen development 95% ranges are sky_field_0016, sky_field_0215, sky_field_0236, sky_field_0256, sky_field_0269, sky_field_0290, sky_field_0332, and sky_field_0422. They remain ordinary validation outcomes and are not deleted. The two fitted-diagnostic results outside its 95% range are sky_field_0249 and sky_field_0256.

For the fitted diagnostic, the corresponding differences are +0.014158, -0.062636, -3.13 percentage points, and +3.06 percentage points. The fitted diagnostic is numerically tighter on these 102 fields, but it remains secondary. That role was fixed before validation, and the development analysis had already shown that a smooth fitted curve can fail badly for an extreme field.

The count-blind environment flag is descriptive only. Flagged validation fields happen to have smaller direct errors than unflagged fields in this fixed sample; this does not show that an environment intersection improves prediction and is not used to remove or reweight any field.

Background control design

Status: Method hierarchy approved; geometry fixture validated Date: 2026-07-18 Issues: BT-005, BT-009, BT-022; interfaces with BT-006, BT-020, and BT-021

Purpose

The background is the expected number and spatial variation of Gaia sources that pass the exact frozen selector but are not members of the target galaxy's compact- stellar-system population. It is not assumed to be spatially uniform, and a missing or unusable control measurement is never represented as zero.

Plain-language overview

Yes: this work starts from the existing catalog of 2,155 nearby galaxies and tries to find suitable comparison patches of sky for each galaxy.

For every galaxy, we want to compare the number of selected Gaia sources near the galaxy with the number in one or more patches that should contain no objects belonging to that galaxy. The comparison patches should be as similar as possible in Milky Way foreground-star density and in how completely and repeatedly Gaia observed the sky. In particular, they should be close to the same Galactic latitude as the galaxy.

This is different from the separate random-sky experiment. The random-sky experiment deliberately samples many Galactic latitudes to measure how foreground contamination changes across the sky. It helps us decide which galaxies can be studied reliably and how closely a comparison patch must match its galaxy. It is not intended to replace the galaxy-specific comparison patches.

In this design, a galaxy-centered region is a circle or annulus centered on the nearby galaxy being studied. A comparison region is an equal-area region moved away from that galaxy and used to estimate unrelated sources. A region described as diagnostic-only is measured to test the method but is not approved for the final background subtraction. When an environment is unresolved, the workflow does not generate comparison coordinates from an unverified group center or size; earlier summaries referred to avoiding such coordinates as “no invented comparison.”

Counts explicitly described as before selection contain Gaia DR3 catalog entries in the stated magnitude range before any UCD classifier or contaminant removal is applied. They are not the sources that survive the point-source, binary, QSO, or other contaminant filters. Counts after the frozen selector is applied will always be labeled separately as selected-source or UCD-candidate counts.

What “similar Gaia observing conditions” means

Gaia is above Earth's atmosphere, so this phrase does not refer to weather. Gaia scanned different sky positions a different number of times and at different scan angles. Source crowding also changes whether Gaia detects a source and whether it publishes a complete astrometric solution. Consequently, two equal-area patches can produce different selected-source counts even when their true populations are otherwise similar.

For this project, a galaxy patch and its comparison patch should be checked using measured field-level quantities that do not use the UCD selector's output:

  • Ecliptic latitude: a simple sky-position indicator related to Gaia's scanning pattern. It is a diagnostic, not a complete description of the scan history.
  • Gaia M10 completeness indicator: the median G magnitude of Gaia sources with no more than ten matched astrometric transits in a small sky patch. A brighter M10 generally indicates shallower or less complete Gaia coverage.
  • Observation-count distributions: quantities such as the number of visibility periods and matched astrometric transits, which describe how often sources in the patch contributed to Gaia measurements.
  • Total Gaia source density: a crowding indicator measured before applying the frozen UCD selector.
  • Query and usable-area status: whether the archive query completed without row truncation and how much of the requested patch has verified completed retrieval.

Galactic latitude is related but separate: it primarily tracks how many Milky Way foreground stars lie in the direction of the patch. Dust and bright-star conditions may be recorded as diagnostics, but they do not remove area from the Gaia measurement.

The exact matching tolerances for these quantities have not been chosen. They must be measured from comparison-patch pairs without looking for the values that produce the largest galaxy overdensity. We also must test which diagnostics materially predict the density of sources passing the frozen selector; a diagnostic that adds no measured predictive value need not become a matching requirement.

The background design has to separate three effects:

  1. Milky Way source-density variation, especially with Galactic latitude;
  2. Gaia sky-position completeness and measurement variation from crowding and the scanning law; and
  3. real extragalactic structure, including other nearby-galaxy or group halos.

Cantat-Gaudin et al. model Gaia DR3 completeness as a function of magnitude and sky position using the local M10 diagnostic, demonstrating that Galactic latitude alone is insufficient (2023A&A...669A..55C, https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09335). Gaia-CRF3 also shows large- and small-scale sky inhomogeneity associated with source density, magnitude, and ecliptic latitude (2022A&A...667A.148G).

Required geometry terms

  • Galaxy-centered radial profile: Counts and usable areas measured in a series of concentric circular rings around the galaxy. The real measurement preserves this profile rather than returning only one number.
  • Central exclusion region: The inner circular part of the galaxy omitted from UCD inference because galaxy substructure, compact star-forming regions, H II regions, and source-extraction artifacts can dominate it. Its calibrated size belongs to BT-021 and is not fixed here.
  • Signal region: The set of radial rings outside the central exclusion that may eventually be combined for an excess measurement. Its outer scale belongs to BT-021 and is not fixed here.
  • Halo exclusion radius: A conservative sky radius inside which a field cannot be treated as background for the host. It is derived from an upper-limit halo radius and a frozen safety factor.
  • Local annulus: A host-centered annulus whose inner edge lies outside the halo exclusion radius. It is distinct from the old fixed 150--300 kpc annulus.
  • Off-target control aperture: A region with the same angular shape and usable area as the signal aperture, displaced away from the host and all screened halos.

  • Large-field model: A model learned from many control fields using sky and Gaia observing-condition predictors. It is a population-level diagnostic, not a substitute for missing local data unless a fallback rule is approved in advance.

  • Excluded background region: A patch of sky that is not allowed to serve as a background comparison because it may contain objects belonging to the target galaxy, another nearby galaxy, a galaxy group, or a cluster. “Excluded” applies only to its use as background; sources there are not deleted from the project.

Kourkchi-Tully catalog terms

  • Principal galaxy: The galaxy the catalog uses as the main or brightest galaxy of a group. It is the group's catalog anchor, not a claim that the galaxy is the only scientifically important member.
  • Isolated principal: A galaxy that the catalog treats as a one-member group: the galaxy is its own principal galaxy and no other cataloged galaxy was assigned to its group. In plain language, this is one ordinary galaxy that this particular catalog did not place in a multi-galaxy group. It does not prove that the galaxy is physically isolated or has no faint satellites.
  • R2t, the estimated second-turnaround radius: A model-based estimate of the size of the more collapsed, gravitationally bound part of a galaxy group. The catalog derives it from the group's K-band luminosity and inferred mass. It is roughly a group-scale halo boundary, not a directly observed sharp edge.
  • R1t, the estimated first-turnaround radius: A model-based estimate of the larger surrounding association where matter has stopped joining the general expansion of the Universe and is beginning to fall inward. It describes a wider region than the settled group core. It is also not a directly observed sharp edge.

The two radii answer different questions. R2t describes the smaller collapsed group region; R1t describes a larger association around one or more groups. That is why neither can be substituted automatically for the other.

Recommended three-layer design

Layer 1: Paired off-target controls — recommended primary

Generate multiple candidate control apertures symmetrically around each host. A control must:

  • match the signal aperture's angular geometry and magnitude/selector contract;
  • lie entirely outside the host halo exclusion radius;
  • satisfy predeclared tolerances in Galactic latitude, ecliptic latitude, and an empirical Gaia completeness indicator such as M10;
  • avoid the buffered halos of other cataloged nearby galaxies, groups, and clusters;
  • retain crowding diagnostics and explicit missing-area or query-truncation states; and
  • receive a permanent accepted or rejected status with a reason.

Symmetric candidates reduce the risk that one preferred direction is chosen after seeing source counts. The number of controls and matching tolerances must be calibrated from control-control comparisons, not selected to minimize a host's background.

Layer 2: Valid host-centered local annulus — robustness method

Use a host-centered annulus only when its inner edge is outside the halo exclusion radius and its full usable area is measured. This method has the strongest local match to Gaia and Milky Way conditions, but it can fail for nearby galaxies, large halos, cluster environments, or neighboring systems. Failure produces an explicit unavailable status, not zero density and not automatic substitution.

Layer 3: Stratified large-field model — population diagnostic

Recompute the Phase III random-field experiment with the frozen selector and exact areas. Stratify or model at least:

  • absolute Galactic latitude;
  • ecliptic latitude;
  • Gaia source density or the DR3 M10 completeness proxy;
  • magnitude distribution within the frozen applicability domain; and
  • masking and query-completeness indicators.

This layer measures residual sky variation, calibrates control-matching tolerances, and supports BT-022's Galactic-latitude decision. It must use spatially separated development and validation fields so that the model's predictive scatter is measured out of sample.

Halo exclusion radius

The current host table contains K-band luminosity and distance but no verified R50, stellar mass, group membership, halo mass, or R200c. Therefore the exclusion radius cannot yet be calculated reproducibly.

For isolated central galaxies, one feasible route is K-band luminosity to stellar mass, then a published stellar-mass--halo-mass relation, then R200c, propagating scatter and using a declared upper envelope. Moster et al. provide a fitted stellar-to-halo relation (2013MNRAS.428.3121M, https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.5807), but it is a central/subhalo population relation rather than a precise mass measurement for an individual galaxy. A size-based cross-check is possible after verified half-light radii exist; Kravtsov reports an approximately linear galaxy-size--halo-radius relation with substantial scatter (2013ApJ...764L..31K, https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.2980).

For satellites and brightest group or cluster galaxies, the containing environment halo—not only the individual galaxy halo—can contaminate a control. These systems require verified central/satellite and group/cluster information. The inherited is_cluster field is not adequate because it is an undocumented sky-circle proxy.

Area and query safeguards

  • Use spherical geometry for all apertures and intersections.
  • Record requested, masked, successfully queried, and usable solid angle separately.
  • Do not impose a fixed angular cap that truncates a required physical region.
  • Detect archive row limits and query truncation; a truncated field fails.
  • Store raw retrieved sources once per sky tile and apply the shared frozen selector locally so target and control denominators remain identical.
  • Preserve one Gaia source with multiple host/control associations rather than silently deduplicating it.
  • Version the selector, geometry, masks, host catalog, environment catalog, and every control disposition in a run manifest.

Statistical interface

BT-005 supplies background counts, usable areas, field-to-field variation, and method differences. BT-006 must then propagate Poisson count uncertainty, background-estimation uncertainty, and between-method disagreement. This design does not approve the old (observed - expected) / sqrt(expected) diagnostic.

Negative excesses remain valid. A host with no valid background measurement remains in the run manifest with an unavailable inferential result rather than being discarded or assigned zero background.

Small-scale validation before retrieval

  1. Build a geometry-only fixture containing a nearby isolated host, a more distant isolated host, a group/cluster central, a satellite, and a host with a nearby contaminating galaxy.
  2. Generate control candidates without querying Gaia.
  3. Verify halo exclusion, constant-area matching, spherical geometry, neighbor screening, and explicit failure states.
  4. Run bounded Gaia queries for one accepted target/control set only after the geometry and metadata contract pass.
  5. Benchmark elapsed time, returned rows, and usable area before scaling.

Decisions required before implementation

  1. Whether paired off-target controls are the primary estimator, with the valid local annulus and large-field model used as robustness methods.
  2. Whether unresolved group/cluster membership should block inferential background assignment while permitting clearly labeled exploratory profiles.
  3. Whether to wait for verified R50 and environment data, or permit a conservative K-luminosity--based upper R200c proxy for isolated centrals in the first fixture.

All three decisions were approved by the project lead on 2026-07-18 as recommended.

Geometry-fixture result

The synthetic, query-free fixture in scripts/phase3_background/validate_background_control_geometry.py contains a nearby isolated host, a more distant isolated host, an unresolved cluster central, an unresolved satellite, and an isolated host with one direction contaminated by a neighbor halo. It produces 20 symmetric control candidates at exactly matched Galactic latitude. Eleven are accepted and nine rejected. All nine predeclared checks pass, including explicit environment blocking, neighbor rejection with alternative controls preserved, and a nearby exclusion radius larger than the old two-degree cap. The maximum numerical Galactic-latitude mismatch is 7.11e-14 degrees.

Artifacts:

The fixture inputs are synthetic geometry values, not estimates for real hosts and not permission to infer a background before the real environment and halo-proxy contracts are validated.

Environment-radius audit

The Kourkchi-Tully accepted associations were tested against the catalog's own group second-turnaround radius, R2t. Among 883 accepted group members, 1.5 R2t encloses 810 (91.73%), 2 R2t encloses 863 (97.73%), and 3 R2t encloses 881 (99.77%). The maximum projected ratio is 7.22. This is an internal catalog- consistency test because both membership and R2t come from the same construction; it is not independent halo validation.

The larger association first-turnaround scale, R1t, is also not a universal replacement. It is usable for 832 accepted group members and encloses 763 (86.41%) at one radius in the published association geometry. Group membership, association membership, and physical halo exclusion must therefore remain distinct. No safety factor is frozen.

Bounded Gaia field-condition result

A first live-archive benchmark used NGC 4698 and two 0.1-degree-radius comparison patches with exactly the same Galactic latitude. It deliberately did not apply or inspect the UCD selector. The target, west, and east patches contained 121, 100, and 122 Gaia sources in the allowed G magnitude range. Their average numbers of visibility periods were 15.98, 12.07, and 14.46, while their M10 values—the median G magnitude of sources with no more than ten matched astrometric transits—were 21.187, 21.093, and 21.102.

All six validation checks passed, including complete retrieval, non-missing observation counts, positive area, and a query contract containing no selector output. The measured run took 11.19 seconds. Same Galactic latitude therefore did not guarantee identical Gaia coverage or source density in this bounded test. One target and two comparisons are insufficient to choose diagnostics or matching tolerances, so no scientific rule is frozen. Full definitions, measurements, and limitations are recorded in docs/background_field_conditions_benchmark.md.

The 0.1-degree radius was chosen only to keep this first archive query small and quick. It is not a host-measurement recommendation. At the measured catalog distances, 0.1 degree corresponds to a projected radius of 4.712394 kpc for ESO 006-G001 but 41.771283 kpc for NGC 4698. A fixed angle therefore cannot provide the planned comparable radial profile across nearby galaxies.

The follow-up count-blind audit covers all 2,155 hosts and preserves an exploratory, recombinable physical-ring view rather than one fixed circular count. A provisional 600 kpc diagnostic radius exceeds two degrees for 1,018 hosts and reaches 13.064418 degrees, so complete retrieval may require multiple archive tiles. The current host table has no R50 field; the central exclusion and normalized radial profile therefore remain uncalibrated. The complete measurement contract is in docs/radial_measurement_design.md.

For large circular regions, the validated query partition uses non-overlapping declination ranges while retaining the exact original circle in every archive request. One bounded NGC 4698 test reconstructed the complete query's 500 unique Gaia identifiers exactly from four ranges, with all nine checks passing. Production queries still require row-count-based splitting, checkpoints, and resume behavior; see docs/gaia_query_partition_validation.md.

The follow-up restart test now measures row counts, recursively splits oversized ranges, writes atomic checkpoints, and resumes from verified files. At that stage, the full-column row limit and masked radial-ring areas had not yet been validated; see docs/gaia_adaptive_retrieval_validation.md.

The subsequent 19-column benchmark succeeded through 10,000 rows. Five-host field-condition result

The same pre-selector measurements were extended to five review hosts using 13 frozen patches. Four resolved environments received east and west test patches; Arp 244 received only a target measurement because its environment association is unresolved. All nine validation checks pass, and the measured run took 46.93 seconds. No test patch is approved as a scientific background.

The one-galaxy catalog cases have M10 ranges of 0.094 and 0.105 magnitude across their three patches. The NGC 1316 and M60 group cases have larger ranges of 0.503 and 0.498 magnitude. M60 also illustrates that total Gaia density is not a pure survey-condition measurement: its target patch contains 289 Gaia sources in the allowed magnitude range, compared with 59 and 130 in the two diagnostic patches. Some of the target excess may be a real M60 or Virgo population. Total Gaia density may therefore diagnose crowding, but it must not be matched so rigidly that a real host-associated population is treated as a field-quality failure. Full definitions and results are in docs/background_review_field_conditions.md.

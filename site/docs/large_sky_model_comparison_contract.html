Large-sky background model comparison

Date: 2026-07-19

Script: scripts/phase3_background/freeze_large_sky_model_comparison.py

Machine-readable contract: data/results/background/large_sky_model_comparison_contract_v1.json

Contract identifier: model_contract_8da80ba5e4bd6d3027e6

What is being predicted

For each field, the measured quantity is the number of sources passing the frozen Gaia selector per square degree of successfully queried area. The model works with the count and exact area together so that fields with more measured area carry the appropriate statistical weight. An incomplete field keeps a failure record and is left out of fitting; it is never changed to a zero count.

Models to compare

The models are ordered from simplest to most flexible:

  1. One average rate for the entire development sky.
  2. A rate that can change smoothly with distance from the Milky Way plane.
  3. A sky-position model that also uses Galactic hemisphere and longitude, plus distance from the ecliptic plane.
  4. The position model plus measured Gaia conditions: total Gaia source density, M10, mean visibility periods, mean matched transits, and three summaries of the G-magnitude distribution.
  5. A limited tree model using the same information. This checks whether simple nonlinear relationships improve prediction enough to justify a less transparent model.
  6. A diagnostic version that adds the count-blind environment flag. This comparison cannot approve or delete a background field and cannot be selected as the main model.

A smooth curve is used where a straight-line relationship would be unnecessarily restrictive. The allowed curve complexity and the limited tree settings are fixed in the machine-readable contract before the 398-field measurements.

How prediction is tested

The 398 development fields belong to 95 broad sky blocks. All fields in one block stay together throughout testing, which prevents nearby fields from appearing on both sides of the same test.

  • An outer grouped fold holds out whole development sky blocks, fits the model on the other blocks, and measures how well it predicts the held-out blocks. Five different outer splits provide the performance estimate.
  • An inner grouped fold operates only inside the current outer training set. Its sole job is to choose among the already declared curve, penalty, or tree settings. Four inner splits are used. Inner results are not reported as final performance.

The main score is mean Poisson deviance, which compares predicted and observed counts while respecting the count nature of the data; lower is better. Supporting checks report typical and root-mean-square log-density errors, residual patterns by Galactic and ecliptic latitude, and whether the stated 68% and 95% prediction ranges contain the expected fractions of held-out fields.

Choosing the final development model

The lowest average outer-fold deviance identifies the measured best score. The chosen model is the simplest candidate whose score is statistically indistinguishable from that best score under the predeclared one-standard-error rule. In plain language, a more complicated model is used only when its improvement is larger than the field-to-field uncertainty in the comparison.

After selection, the development fit, all data transformations, and the method for constructing prediction ranges are frozen. Only then may the 102 held-back fields be opened once for a final check. They cannot be used to revise the model.

Current status

The frozen comparison has now been run on all 398 development fields. The selected model combines sky position with measured Gaia conditions and uses three spline knots with alpha = 0.1. All 22 fitting safeguards pass. The extreme flagged field sky_field_0317 is retained and documented rather than deleted; it dominates one outer fold and limits the model's applicability in unusual crowded or physically associated regions. No validation selector result has been opened, and no large-sky field has been approved as a scientific background. See docs/large_sky_development_model_comparison.md.

After reviewing the extreme-field failure, this fitted model was reassigned on 2026-08-11 to a secondary diagnostic role. It can describe smooth trends, but it does not supply the primary background value. The primary large-sky reference is now the preserved distribution of directly measured development fields in the same broad absolute-Galactic-latitude band. See docs/large_sky_direct_background_comparison.md.

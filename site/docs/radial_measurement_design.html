Radial measurement and central exclusion design

Status: First-stage conservative profile policy approved; scientific boundaries not final Date: 2026-08-11 Issues: BT-005, BT-020, BT-021

What the real measurement does

For each nearby galaxy, the pipeline will keep every retrieved source's continuous angular separation from the galaxy center. Using the galaxy distance, it will also store projected separation in kiloparsecs. When a verified half-light radius, R50, is available, it will additionally store separation divided by R50.

The pipeline will then summarize the sources in a series of concentric circular rings. Each ring has an inner radius and an outer radius. Together, the rings form a radial profile showing how selected-source density changes with distance from the galaxy. The stored continuous separations allow the rings to be combined or changed without repeating the Gaia query.

The following regions have different roles:

  • Central exclusion: The innermost galaxy region. Its sources remain in the raw data and contamination summaries, but do not contribute to UCD inference.
  • Radial measurement rings: Successive rings outside the center. Their counts, usable areas, and densities form the host profile.
  • Off-galaxy comparison rings: The same angular ring shapes placed away from the galaxy. They estimate unrelated sources after halo and environment screening.
  • Outer retrieval boundary: The largest radius downloaded around the galaxy. It must cover every requested ring and must not be silently shortened by a fixed angular cap.

Exploratory ring view

The first recombinable physical-radius view uses edges at 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200, 300, and 600 kpc. These values preserve the union of the existing fine inner pilot grid and the earlier 600 kpc morphology diagnostic. They are not an approved central exclusion, final signal range, or local-background definition. In particular, the 0--5 kpc ring is not automatically accepted as UCD signal.

Every output must retain exact requested area, masked area, successfully queried area, and usable area for each ring. Areas use spherical geometry. Missing coverage is an explicit failure or partial-area state, never a zero count over the requested area.

Geometry audit

The current host catalog contains 2,155 positive distances but no R50 or equivalent half-light-radius field. For a provisional 600 kpc outer diagnostic radius, the measured angular radius ranges from 1.374835 to 13.064418 degrees, with a median of 1.951086 degrees. It exceeds two degrees for 1,018 hosts, five degrees for 123 hosts, and ten degrees for 25 hosts.

Therefore the inherited two-degree cap is invalid. Large nearby-host retrievals will require tiled or otherwise complete archive queries with explicit coverage and row-limit checks. The 600 kpc value remains exploratory; this audit does not approve it as the final outer signal boundary.

A bounded live test validates the first gap-free retrieval building block. A 0.2-degree-radius NGC 4698 circle returned 500 Gaia entries both as one complete query and as four non-overlapping horizontal sky strips. The strip counts were 82, 154, 149, and 115; their 500 unique source identifiers match the complete-circle reference exactly. All nine checks pass in 12.96 seconds. Real strip sizes will be set from measured archive row counts rather than this test's 0.1-degree height. Production checkpointing and adaptive splitting remain pending. Full details are in docs/gaia_query_partition_validation.md.

The restartable implementation has now passed a second bounded test. A test-only 130-entry limit divided the same 500-entry circle into six measured strips. The first pass stopped after two saved strips; a fresh run skipped those two and retrieved the remaining four. A deliberately damaged checkpoint was then detected and replaced without repeating the other five downloads. All 15 checks pass in 21.68 seconds. The production safe row limit and a full-selector-column restart benchmark remain pending; see docs/gaia_adaptive_retrieval_validation.md.

The full 19-column retrieval benchmark subsequently completed tests from 100 to 10,000 rows. Three 5,000-row runs returned identical identifiers in 1.948628--1.976649 seconds and serialized to 1,027,468 bytes each. Use 5,000 rows as the provisional development checkpoint limit before the multi-field test. The limit is not an archive maximum.

The full-column restart test now covers NGC 4698, M60, and ESO 006-G001 with 2,991, 6,497, and 13,010 measured rows. They require one, two, and four final strips, respectively, and all 13 checks pass in 29.589341 seconds. Freeze 5,000 rows as the per-strip limit for development and pilot retrieval under the current 19-column contract.

The spherical area component now records requested, successfully queried, missing, masked, and usable area for every ring. It derives query coverage from verified completed strips and subtracts the union of circular masks, so overlapping masks are not counted twice. Complete strip plans for all three performance fields reproduce the requested area; a controlled partial M60 state retains positive missing area. The masks used to test generic intersection geometry are explicitly synthetic and cannot be used for Gaia science. The Gaia area policy applies no bright-star, dust, or ancillary-imaging masks: usable area equals successfully queried area, while an incomplete or failed strip contributes missing area. All 18 checks pass in 0.216274 seconds. See docs/radial_ring_area_accounting.md.

The count-blind audit uses no Gaia source or selector counts and passes all seven validation checks. Its artifacts are:

  • data/results/background/radial_measurement_geometry_audit_v1.csv
  • data/results/background/radial_measurement_geometry_audit_v1.json

Reproduce it with:

uv run python scripts/phase3_background/audit_radial_measurement_geometry.py

Calibrating the central exclusion

The central boundary must be calibrated once for a declared host population, not chosen separately for each galaxy after viewing its UCD excess.

The calibration will use development galaxies to measure, as a function of radius divided by R50:

  • the density of compact H II and star-forming-region references where available;
  • the density of very blue sources and clearly extended galaxy substructure;
  • source-extraction failures, bright-core masks, crowding, and missing Gaia area;
  • the radial distribution and retained fraction of reliable literature UCDs; and
  • sensitivity to the photometric band and uncertainty of the measured R50.

A candidate central boundary is acceptable only if it materially reduces central contamination while retaining a declared fraction of reliable reference UCDs and remaining stable on spatially separate validation galaxies. It must not be chosen because it produces the largest target overdensity.

No central multiplier is approved now. The earlier 2 x R50 example remains only a value to test. Until R50 coverage is completed under BT-020, a host can retain its physical-kpc exploratory profile but cannot receive a final normalized central exclusion from an undocumented estimate.

For the first profile stage, label 0--50 kpc as a conservative contamination region and withhold it from UCD inference. This deliberately large physical exclusion is a temporary safety rule, not the final central boundary. Preserve its component rings and test the already declared 20, 25, 30, 40, and 50 kpc edges using contamination behavior. Do not choose among them by maximizing a UCD excess. When a verified R50 becomes available, report the normalized profile as well; otherwise mark only that view unavailable.

Calibrating the outer signal range

The final outer signal boundary will also be common to a declared host class rather than optimized per galaxy. Development profiles will test where the selected-source excess becomes consistent with the independently measured background, while validation profiles will measure whether that boundary generalizes. Group and cluster cases retain the same host-centered profile, but their scientific background remains unavailable when the containing environment cannot be screened.

The final analysis may combine several rings only after the central exclusion, outer signal boundary, background method, and handling of the resulting radial scan have been declared.

The first-stage retrieval should retain the complete physical profile through the declared 600 kpc edge wherever verified retrieval coverage permits. Review the measured outer profiles before choosing a smaller final signal boundary. A failed or incomplete outer strip remains missing area and cannot silently shorten the requested profile or become a zero-density ring.

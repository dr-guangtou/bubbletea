Held-back off-target validation result

Status: One-time held-back check complete; no field approved as a scientific background Date: 2026-07-19 Issues: BT-005, BT-009

What was tested

The four off-target circles kept out of development were opened after the selector and summary rules were fixed. The run used the same 19 Gaia columns, G-magnitude range, selector version, probability threshold, 0.1-degree circle, usable-area definition, east/west host pairing, and counting-only ranges used for development. No setting was changed after the validation counts became visible.

Here, validation means a one-time check on circles whose selector probabilities, decisions, and passing-source counts were not used to choose the procedure. Earlier measurements of total Gaia source density and observing conditions at these positions were allowed and remain separate.

A source that passes the selector is a retained search candidate, not a confirmed UCD. These circles are diagnostic measurements and are not approved backgrounds for a host-galaxy measurement.

Field results

Each circle has an exact spherical area of 0.031416 square degree. For this Gaia measurement, usable area equals successfully queried area; no bright-star, dust, or ancillary-imaging masks are applied.

Comparison with development

The combined validation density is 1.181818 times the combined development density of 350.140964 per square degree. It falls outside the development 68% counting-only range but inside the development 95% range. This is a description, not a rule that the result must pass.

Individual development fields span 190.985980–572.957941 per square degree, while validation fields span 95.492990–604.788937. Development host pairs span 286.478970–413.802957, while validation host pairs span 270.563472–557.042442. Both pairs of ranges overlap.

The result supports the limited claim that the fixed procedure can be executed on untouched fields without an obvious incompatible total density. It also shows large field-to-field variation. Four validation circles from two host systems are therefore too few and too small to define a survey-wide background model, matching tolerance, or Galactic-latitude limit.

Safeguards and artifacts

All 24 safeguards pass. They verify the frozen field identities, exclusion of all development fields from validation queries and outputs, complete retrieval, unchanged selector and area rules, count and area conservation, the fixed seven-row summary structure, unchanged development files, and the explicitly descriptive status of every comparison.

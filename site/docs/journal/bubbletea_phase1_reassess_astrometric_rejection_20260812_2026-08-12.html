date: 2026-08-12 repo: bubbletea branch: phase1_reassess_astrometric_rejection_20260812 tags: - journal - phase1 - selector - astrometry

Progress

  • Froze the development-only astrometric-rejection contract before calculating any retention-versus-rejection result.
  • Applied the unchanged selector version 1 first and measured 1,885 exact boundaries using the larger available parallax or proper-motion significance.
  • Preserved missing parallax and proper motion as unknown; sources with both missing were not rejected by the second rule.
  • Measured that the 3-standard-deviation literature reference lowers reliable-UCD retention from 135/146 to 131/146 while removing one retained ordinary star and one retained QSO.
  • Verified that every measured boundary retaining at least 90% of all reliable UCDs leaves the equal-weight priority-contaminant retention unchanged from selector version 1.
  • Preserved the five reliable UCD associations above 3 standard deviations in a source-level review table; all five originate in the Voggel et al. NGC 5128 catalog.
  • Generated the full tradeoff curve and companion figure; all 19 deterministic validation checks and task-specific Ruff and pre-commit checks passed.

Lessons Learned

  • Large Gaia motion significance is a useful warning for individual review but is not a safe universal rejection rule for barely resolved UCDs.
  • A contaminant-cohort retention fraction does not estimate real-field catalog purity because ordinary stars are vastly more common than genuine UCDs.
  • A real-host positive control must separate an unbiased contamination-audit sample from a deliberately promising new-candidate shortlist.

Key Issues

  • The approved recommendation is to keep selector version 1 unchanged and retain significant astrometry as an explicit warning rather than a hard rejection.
  • M60 is the next positive-control host. Its two reliable confirmed UCDs test end-to-end recovery but are development objects, so their recovery is not an independent selector validation.
  • The M60 branch must freeze its radial geometry, known-object recovery list, representative contamination sampling, and candidate-ranking rules before the full passing-source list or ancillary images are inspected.
  • Star, non-single-star, QSO, galaxy, and other contaminant treatments require separate plain-language definitions and project-lead discussion before any exclusion is implemented.

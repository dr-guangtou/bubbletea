Radial-ring area accounting

Status: Spherical area component validated; Gaia no-mask policy fixed Date: 2026-07-18 Issues: BT-005, BT-010, BT-021

Plain-language definitions

Each radial ring now carries five separate areas:

  • Requested area: the entire ring that the measurement asked for.
  • Successfully queried area: the part covered by retrieval strips whose saved files passed all completion and integrity checks.
  • Missing area: the requested part without a verified completed strip.
  • Masked area: the union of explicitly unusable regions inside the successfully queried area. Overlapping masks are counted once, not once per mask.
  • Usable area: successfully queried area minus masked area. This is the denominator for a source density.

An interrupted query therefore produces a positive missing area rather than a false zero source density. A complete query can still have less usable area than requested area if an authoritative mask removes part of the sky.

The central galaxy exclusion is a different concept. It marks inner rings as diagnostic-only for the scientific analysis; it does not pretend that those rings were missing from the archive or geometrically unusable.

For the Gaia measurement, masked area is always zero. Bright-star, dust, and ancillary-imaging masks are not applied. Therefore Gaia usable area equals successfully queried area. Crowding, observation counts, or dust may still be recorded as diagnostics, but they do not remove Gaia sky area.

Spherical calculation

The requested area is the analytic area between two circles on a sphere. Retrieval strips and circular masks are intersected with each ring on the sphere, including right-ascension wraparound and polar geometry. The intersections are integrated to an internal absolute tolerance of 1e-13 steradian and validated to 1e-8 square degree. Here, "exact area" means spherical rather than a flat-sky approximation, with the numerical integration error recorded in every row.

Each mask must have an identifier, center, angular radius, reason, and provenance. The area code forms the union of all masks before subtraction, so overlapping masks cannot remove the same sky twice.

Bounded validation

The measured retrieval plans for NGC 4698, M60, and ESO 006-G001 were divided into four test rings each. A controlled M60 case retains only one of its two completed strips. These are fractions of the archive performance-test circles, not physical- kiloparsec rings or approved scientific apertures.

For all three completed retrievals, the successfully queried area reproduces the requested spherical area. The largest ring-level difference is 1.873002e-12 square degree, and the four rings for each host sum to the analytic circle area within 1.110223e-16 square degree.

Under the Gaia policy, the complete M60 fixture has 1.767121 square degrees both successfully queried and usable. The controlled one-of-two-strip state has 0.884062 square degree both successfully queried and usable, while 0.883059 square degree remains explicitly missing.

Two overlapping circular masks were then placed in the M60 fixture. They are explicitly labeled synthetic_fixture_not_survey_coverage; they are not real Gaia, bright-star, dust, or imaging masks. The complete fixture contains 1.767121 square degrees of requested and successfully queried area. The union of the two masks removes 0.039362 square degree, leaving 1.727758 square degrees usable. A controlled partial state with only one of two retrieval strips complete records 0.884062 square degree queried and 0.883059 square degree missing.

All 18 predeclared checks pass in 0.216274 seconds. They cover completed and partial retrievals, area identities, overlapping-mask union, non-negative areas, polar geometry, central-ring disposition, retrieval-plan counts, the Gaia no-mask policy, and the synthetic-mask provenance warning. The output contains 24 ring records.

Boundary between Gaia and ancillary imaging

Real Gaia host densities must not use the synthetic fixture masks. The generic mask intersection remains available only as tested geometry for a future data product that explicitly requires masks. Any later ancillary-imaging measurement has its own coverage and quality policy; it does not change the Gaia denominator.

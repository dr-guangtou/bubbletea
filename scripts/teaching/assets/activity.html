The scientific question

How can a compact galaxy hide among stars?

An ultra-compact dwarf galaxy (UCD) is a compact stellar system, sometimes a very massive star cluster and sometimes the surviving center of a stripped galaxy. The boundary is not sharp. Gaia detects compact concentrations of light, not physical identities: a compact galaxy or bright galactic center can enter a catalog designed around stars.

A massive black hole was found in M60-UCD1 through detailed follow-up. That makes it a compelling clue to galaxy evolution - not something you can establish from this worksheet alone. Seth et al. 2014 ↗

Measurements, not a verdict.

One milliarcsecond (mas) is 0.001 arcsec. Motion is in mas/year. Renormalized unit weight error (RUWE) measures astrometric fit quality; excess noise is extra angular scatter, not size. C = (I_BP + I_RP) / I_G uses blue, red, and broad-band fluxes, not magnitudes. Missing means not supplied, never zero.
